The workbook: practise the 2026 law

No past paper has yet been set under the Nigeria Tax Act 2025, so this page supplies the practice. Eight questions are modelled on the patterns of the last twelve diets, with full answers under the new law. There's also a drill for the examiner's favourite trap, flashcards for every number, and mark-ready answer frames for the theory topics that keep returning.

Read before you start

These questions are original, written for practice; they are not ICAN questions. Where the 2026 Study Text leaves a method open (for example, how the residue of a pre-2026 asset is spread, or where a cost-price-ratio restriction is absorbed), the answer states its assumption. In the exam, write the same kind of note: it earns credit and protects you if the marker takes a different view.

Use the 15 minutes' reading time
  1. Read every requirement first, then the Section B and C scenarios. Pick your four optional questions now.
  2. Prefer the computations you have drilled: the hydrocarbon tax question in B and the gains question in C have appeared almost every diet.
  3. In Question 1, mark every expense line with A (add back), OK or D (deduct), and note each date's year of assessment and which law applies.
  4. Note any part that asks for theory: those marks are the fastest to earn.
While writing
  1. Start with the question you know best. A confident first answer settles you.
  2. Stop at the time budget. The first 60% of a question's marks takes half the time; the last marks take the rest.
  3. Report or memo heading, introduction, summary table, then appendices. Each carries marks.
  4. One point per mark in theory parts, each starting with a bold key phrase.
  5. If you are stuck on a figure, state an assumption and move on. Later lines can still score.
Company tax order
  1. Basis period and YOA
  2. Adjusted profit (A / OK / D)
  3. Chargeable gains (separate working)
  4. Capital allowances (class, life, transition)
  5. Total profit = assessable + gains − loss − CA
  6. CIT 30%, levy 4% of assessable
  7. Minimum ETR test, credits, WHT
Hydrocarbon tax order
  1. Licence (PML 30% / PPL 15%) and terrain
  2. Revenue at fiscal price × barrels × rate
  3. Allowed costs; list disallowed with reasons
  4. CPR working (A, B, C1, C2, D)
  5. Adjusted → losses (same class) → assessable
  6. Less CA and production allowance
  7. HT on chargeable profit
Gains order
  1. Is it a chargeable asset and disposal? Exempt?
  2. Date of disposal → YOA
  3. Proceeds (or market value)
  4. Less cost or TWDV, plus incidental costs
  5. Part disposal A ÷ (A + B)
  6. Reliefs: replacement, reinvestment, instalments
  7. Add to total profit at 30% (old law: 10% CGT)

Q4 · Zuma Ceramics Ltd: chargeable gainsSection C style · 15 marks · 27 minutes

Zuma Ceramics Ltd (31 December year end) had these transactions in 2026, in ₦ million:

  1. Land bought in 2021 for 90 plus 2 legal and stamp costs. Part sold in April 2026 for 75; agent's fee 1.5. The unsold part is worth 125.
  2. A warehouse (cost 60, TWDV 38) burnt down. Insurance of 50 was received in May 2026 and scrap sold for 2. A replacement was completed in September 2026 for 70.
  3. Shares in a listed Nigerian company, cost 140, sold in March 2026 for 200. In November 2026, 150 was reinvested in shares of another Nigerian company.
  4. A plot bought for 12 was compulsorily acquired by the State in June 2026 for 30. The company had no prior knowledge of the plan.

The remaining instalment gains (12 and 12) follow the 2027 and 2028 receipts into later years of assessment. Any instalment later shown to be irrecoverable is adjusted.
(b) Replacement warehouse

Compensation plus scrap (52) is below the replacement cost (70), so there is no gain. The shortfall of 18 is treated as a new qualifying addition. Allowances continue on the old residue of 38. The allowance base is 38 + 18 = 56, not 70, at class 1 (10%).

Gains of 70 at the 30% CIT rate add ₦21m of tax for 2027. Under the old CGT Act the same gains would have cost ₦7m at 10%. Gains are no longer a cheap separate charge, which changes planning around disposals.

Kano Leather Ltd, a Nigerian company, has a branch in Ghana (no DTA with Nigeria). For the year ended 31 December 2026 (₦ million): Nigerian adjusted profit 300; Ghana branch adjusted profit 100 (after adding back Ghanaian tax). Ghanaian tax paid on the branch profit: 25. Capital allowances: Nigeria 40, branch 10.

The branch is part of the Nigerian company, so all its profit is taxed here. Foreign tax is never deducted as an expense. Claim the credit within 2 years after the year of assessment. Had Ghanaian tax been 35, the credit would stop at 27.
(b) Old s.44 Commonwealth relief

Ghanaian rate 25 ÷ 100 = 25%, which exceeds half the Nigerian rate (15%), so relief was limited to 15% × branch total profit 90 = 13.5. The NTA credit of 25 relieves the whole foreign tax, because it compares actual taxes rather than half-rates, and it now applies to any country, not just the Commonwealth.

Theory answer bank

Mark-ready point lists for the discussion parts that keep returning. Learn the bold phrase of each point; in the hall, write the phrase and one sentence of explanation.

Asked Nov 2021, May 2025

Back duty audit

  1. Purpose: re-examine past returns where errors, omissions or deliberate understatement are suspected.
  2. Time limit: additional assessments within 6 years; none for fraud or deliberate misstatement (s.36 NTAA).
  3. Process: notification, records examined, tax recomputed and compared with returns filed.
  4. Result: shortfall assessed with penalty and interest at the CBN rate.
  5. Rights: the taxpayer may object (30 days) and appeal to the TAT.
  6. Records: books must be kept at least 6 years, in English or with certified translation.
Asked May 2026

Roles of the Vienna Convention

  1. Codifies interpretation: a uniform approach for courts and tax authorities.
  2. Good faith, ordinary meaning, context, object and purpose (art. 31); supplementary means (art. 32).
  3. Pacta sunt servanda: treaties bind and must be performed in good faith (art. 26).
  4. No internal-law excuse for failing to perform a treaty (art. 27).
  5. Rules for making, amending and terminating treaties.
  6. Consistency and predictability in applying DTAs; supports MAP and arbitration.
Asked Nov 2025

TIWB, cooperative compliance, simultaneous examination

  1. TIWB: OECD–UNDP; experts audit alongside local officials; host country leads; builds capacity, does not replace staff.
  2. Cooperative compliance: trust, transparency and mutual understanding with large taxpayers.
  3. Taxpayer keeps a tax control framework and discloses risks early; authority gives timely certainty.
  4. Benefits: fewer disputes, lower cost.
  5. Simultaneous examination: two or more countries examine one taxpayer at the same time, independently, sharing information.
  6. Challenges: coordination, confidentiality, legal complexity.
Asked Nov 2022, May 2023, Nov 2025

Transfer pricing compliance

  1. Arm's length principle for every controlled transaction.
  2. Declaration form by 30 June: entity, parent, directors, shareholders, structure, connected persons, auditors, consultant, secretary.
  3. Disclosure form by 30 September: income and costs of controlled transactions, summary, method, financials.
  4. TP policy and compliance report: burden of proof on the taxpayer.
  5. Penalties: ₦10m + ₦10k a day; higher of ₦10m or 1%; ₦25k a day for updates.
  6. Disputes: Head of TP Unit within 30 days → Decision Review Panel → TAT; APAs and MAP.
Asked Nov 2021, Nov 2025

BEPS: techniques and OECD strategies

  1. IP and technology licensing through low-tax entities.
  2. Thin capitalisation: debt instead of equity.
  3. Hybrid mismatches: double non-taxation or double deduction.
  4. Entities without substance in preferential regimes.
  5. OECD responses: hybrid rules, CFC rules, interest limits, treaty-abuse rules, PE changes.
  6. TP aligned to value creation; CbCR; MAP; the multilateral instrument.
Asked Nov 2020, May 2025

Tax havens

  1. No or nominal tax on relevant income.
  2. No effective exchange of information.
  3. Lack of transparency.
  4. No substantial activity required.
  5. Counter-measures: CFC rules, TP and thin cap rules, exit charges.
  6. Automatic exchange (CRS, FATCA), blacklists, defensive WHT, substance tests.
Asked Nov 2022, May 2025

Mergers and reorganisations

  1. Merger: no commencement or cessation; no gain; assets at residue.
  2. Unused allowances, losses and WHT credits pass to the survivor.
  3. Sale causing cessation: allowances and losses lost; CA on sale value.
  4. No VAT on transfers in a restructuring.
  5. Related parties: related 1 year before; no disposal within 1 year after; breach revokes.
  6. Tax authority consent and clearance before completion.

National Tax Policy

  1. Features: equity and fairness; simplicity, certainty and clarity; convenience; low compliance cost; low administration cost; flexibility; sustainability.
  2. Government: review laws; quarterly revenue data; funding and autonomy; 3–6 month transition.
  3. Taxpayer: civic duty; right to information, objection, fair appeal, accredited agent.
  4. Revenue agencies: customer focus, cooperation, timely audits, education, anti-corruption.
  5. Professionals: code of conduct; no aiding evasion; promote compliance.

NEITI

  1. Vision: an accountable, effective, well-resourced, result-oriented NEITI.
  2. Mission: transparency, accountability, due process and zero tolerance for corruption in the extractive industries.
  3. Due process and transparency in payments to government.
  4. Accountability in revenue receipts.
  5. Eliminate corruption in determining, paying, receiving and posting revenue.
  6. Conformity with EITI principles.
Asked Nov 2020, Nov 2025

Objections and appeals (NTAA)

  1. Written objection within 30 days of service.
  2. Contents: issues and amounts, amendment sought, justification, profit and tax admitted.
  3. Authority responds within 90 days or the objection is upheld.
  4. Appeal to the TAT within 30 days; grounds as in the objection.
  5. Federal High Court on points of law within 30 days; then Court of Appeal and Supreme Court.
  6. ADR and the Tax Ombud as alternatives.
Expected under the 2026 syllabus

Economic development incentive certificate

  1. Apply to the Secretary of NIPC with capital commitment, QCE plans, production date, products, capital structure, ownership.
  2. Fee 0.1% of QCE, maximum ₦5m.
  3. NIPC → Minister → President approves.
  4. Production day certified by the Industrial Inspectorate within a month.
  5. NRS certifies QCE; below the sector minimum, the certificate is discountenanced.
  6. Credit 5% a year for 5 years; separate books; cannot reduce the 15% top-up.
Asked May 2025, Nov 2025

Mining law points

  1. Possession: own title area, a permit to possess or purchase, or an authorised agent (s.93).
  2. Purchase only with a licence (s.94); small-scale output to licensed buying centres (s.95).
  3. Fees: annual service fee; 30 days' default notice, then revocation.
  4. Land: mining has priority and is an overriding public interest; the Governor revokes occupancy rights within 60 days.
  5. Royalty deductible, administered by the NRS.
  6. Closure fund contributions deductible if cash-backed with independent trustees.
Asked May 2026

ESG in the extractive industries

  1. Fines under the Climate Change Act 2021 and regulators' directives.
  2. Litigation and regulatory audits.
  3. Loss of operating licences.
  4. Reputational damage and loss of investor trust.
  5. Supply chain and operational disruption.
  6. Redress through ADR as well as the courts.
Asked Nov 2023, May 2024, May 2026

Tax practice: legal duties and risks

  1. ICAN code: no business that impairs integrity, objectivity or reputation.
  2. Accreditation as a tax practitioner when acting for reward.
  3. IESBA principles: integrity, objectivity, competence and due care, confidentiality, professional behaviour.
  4. Risks: lack of expertise, inattention, poor documentation, unpredictable income.
  5. Mitigation: work within competence, review, engagement letters, insurance, CPD.
  6. Safeguards: profession, firm and engagement level.
